Embed a live engineering calculator

An embed is a small window onto a CivilKit Calcs sheet that sits on your own web page: the reader changes the inputs and the results recalculate on the spot, with the workings shown. You add it with one line of HTML. It is free, MIT licensed and has no tracking: the calculation runs in the reader's browser and nothing is sent to a server.

A live example

This is the simply supported beam template, embedded exactly as the snippet below embeds it. Drag the span slider or type a load.

The link at its top right, Open in CivilKit Calcs, opens the same sheet in the full app in a new tab, where the reader can edit it, save it and export a report.

How to add one

Copy the snippet of a calculator below into your page's HTML. That is all: no account, no key, no script of your own.

<iframe src="https://civilkit.io/calcs/?embed=1&sample=template-simply-supported-beam" width="100%" height="560" style="border:1px solid #ccc" loading="lazy" title="simply supported beam check: CivilKit Calcs" allow="" sandbox="allow-scripts allow-same-origin allow-popups allow-popups-to-escape-sandbox"></iframe>

Or let a small script place the frames and fit their height to the content: put a placeholder where each calculator goes and load the script once (about 3 KB, no dependencies).

<div data-civilkit-sample="template-simply-supported-beam" data-height="560" data-theme="auto"></div>
<script src="https://civilkit.io/calcs/embed.js" async></script>

Options

Add these to the address of the frame (or as data attributes of the placeholder):

  • theme=light, theme=dark or theme=auto (the default follows the reader's system setting).
  • mode=input (only the form), mode=results (only the workings) or mode=both (the default: the form beside or above the results).
  • hide=title leaves out the sheet's title.
  • embed=edit in place of embed=1 adds a small text editor under the results, for readers who want to change the sheet itself.

Sandboxing

The snippets carry sandbox="allow-scripts allow-same-origin allow-popups allow-popups-to-escape-sandbox". It keeps the frame away from your page (no top navigation, no forms on your origin) while letting the sheet calculate and the Open in CivilKit Calcs link open a normal tab. The frame asks for no browser permissions (allow=""). Python blocks never run in an embed unless the address says allowpython=1, and even then the reader is asked first.

Talking to the frame

A page can set inputs and read results with postMessage. The frame answers only its own parent page and checks every value.

  • Send {type: 'civilkit-set', values: {L: 8}} to set inputs by name. A number must be finite and within the slider's range; a choice must be one of its options. Anything else is refused with {type: 'civilkit-error', errors: [...]}.
  • After each calculation the frame sends {type: 'civilkit-results', values: {name: {value, unit, text}}}.
  • It also sends {type: 'civilkit-ready', inputs: [...]} with the inputs and their ranges, and {type: 'civilkit-embed-height', height} whenever its height changes.

Check that a message comes from https://civilkit.io before you use it. The embedding guide has a complete example.

Free, open and private

CivilKit Calcs is free and open source under the MIT licence, so you may embed it anywhere, commercial sites included. The embed sets no cookies, stores nothing, loads no analytics and sends nothing anywhere: the engine runs in the reader's browser. Every embed links back to the full app with Open in CivilKit Calcs. You can also host your own copy from the source on GitHub.
